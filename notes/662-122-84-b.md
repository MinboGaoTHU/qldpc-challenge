# [[662,122,84]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 331

At row weight 26 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 662` and `k >= 122` is 80, at `[[662,122,80]]` and row weight 24. This entry reaches 84. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 84, at `[[662,122,84]]` and row weight 30.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 61 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 331` with `ord_2(331) = 30`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^331 - 1` over GF(2) number 12 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 3 cosets of total degree 61, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 12 and `b` has weight 14.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^331 - 1) = 61`, `deg gcd(b, x^331 - 1) = 61` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^331 - 1) = 61`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 84. The per-seed spread: 2 seeds found 84, 5 seeds found 85, 10 seeds found 86, 4 seeds found 87, 3 seeds found 88. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 61 whose zeros are the 270 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 35, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 36 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 91. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 85.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 84, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends

- [[662,122,84]] from the same front reaches the same 84 but at row weight 30 against 26 here, so this entry dominates it. It is filed as the -b slug rather than overwriting that entry, which belongs to another contributor, and the board's own pruning decides whether the heavier one stays.
- The sweep row printed 92 and the single-block search cut it to 91 before any gate ran, which looked like the binding constraint at this dimension. The general gate then found 84, below the circulant floor, so the single-block mechanism is not what limits this code after all and the 91 is a fact about one block rather than the distance.
- The second draw at this design point, k=120 at row weight 30, gated to 84 as well and that is one below its own threshold of 85, so it is dead. The two differ only in dimension and the extra two logical qubits are what make this one stand.
- A follow-up sweep of 133 draws at degrees 60 and 61 looking for a lighter pair reached row weight 28 and no further, so row weight 26 here is close to the light end this design point offers.

The reading before this gate was 91, against a gated 84. Every member of this front moved the same way, which is why all of them were gated rather than filed off the sweep.

## Tools

Claude Opus 5 driving a Claude Code session. Distance readings and single-block searches come from the repository's `verify/gf2_fast` extension; divisor assembly, coset selection and the information-set draws are session tooling outside this repository, described above in enough detail to rewrite.

## Reproduction

`m = 331`, `n = 2m = 662`. With `a = {30, 32, 50, 57, 58, 85, 181, 195, 260, 268, 325, 326}` and `b = {33, 36, 41, 45, 49, 95, 101, 213, 224, 228, 256, 284, 306, 321}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/662-122-84-b.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
