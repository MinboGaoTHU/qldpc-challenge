# [[446,76,50]]: cyclic generalized-bicycle code from a designed divisor ideal at m = 223

At row weight 22 or lighter, the best distance on the board with `n <= 446` and `k >= 76` is 31, at `[[390,82,31]]` and row weight 16. This entry reaches 50. Allowing any row weight, the board's best in the same `n` and `k` range is 38, at `[[390,82,38]]` and row weight 32.

## Direction & hypothesis

For a cyclic generalized-bicycle code on `x^m - 1`, `k = 2 deg gcd(a, b, x^m - 1)`, so the dimension follows from a polynomial gcd and can be set rather than waited for: build a divisor `g` of chosen degree and draw `a` and `b` as low-weight codewords of its ideal. Two mechanisms cap this family from opposite ends. A proper divisor `t` of `m` gives a quotient-fold ceiling of `(m/t) d_t`, which binds at low dimension, and the single-block cyclic code of dimension `deg g` carries a light logical inside one circulant block at high dimension. Degree 38 sits where both are weak here.

## What was searched

`m = 223` with `ord_2(223) = 37`, so the cyclotomic cosets of `x^223 - 1` over GF(2) number 7 and the reachable divisor degrees are the subset sums of their sizes. The divisor used here is 2 cosets of total degree 38, chosen among the reachable picks at that degree to maximise the longest run of consecutive residues it leaves out. `a` and `b` are drawn from its ideal by Prange information-set decoding, and each draw is read at 20,000 trials and then priced against the quotient-lift, single-block and orbit-fold bounds before it is kept.

The divisor carries the root-1 coset, so both polynomial weights are even by construction and `(x + 1)` divides the common gcd by design rather than by accident. Here `a` has weight 12 and `b` has weight 10.

## Evidence trail

`deg gcd(a, x^223 - 1) = 38`, `deg gcd(b, x^223 - 1) = 38` and `deg gcd(a, b, x^223 - 1) = 38`, so the leak between the single-block kernels and `k` is 0.

A 24-seed gate at 2,000,000 information-set trials per seed settled at 50. The per-seed spread: 3 seeds found 50, 11 seeds found 52, 8 seeds found 53, 1 seed found 54, 1 seed found 55. The single-block logicals form the cyclic code of dimension 38 whose zeros are the 185 residues the divisor leaves out; the longest run of consecutive residues among them is 27, so the Bose-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem bound puts the lightest single-block logical at 28 or above, and a direct search confined to one circulant block at 12 seeds of 600,000 trials independently finds nothing below 57. The orbit fold over every equal-orbit partition of the qubits returns nothing lighter than the claim. A single uninterrupted run of 8,000,000 trials, matching the depth the verifier itself reaches, reads 50.

The claim is a witness-backed upper bound, not certified. Both witnesses are in the submitted JSON, commute with the opposite check matrix and lie outside its row space; each weighs 50, and the claim is the lighter of the two.

## Dead ends


- Three draws were gated in this cell. This one at row weight 22 settled at 50; a row-weight-24 draw settled at 49 and is beaten on both axes; a second weight-22 draw from a later sweep is still gating and reads 51, which would displace this entry if it holds there.
- The sweeps that produced this draw were spending about ten minutes per draw on field setup at this modulus, a linear exponentiation where square-and-multiply belongs. After that was fixed the same point produced 180 rows in 25 minutes and a better front, so this entry comes from a thin sample and the cell is worth revisiting.

The reading before this gate was 57, against a gated 50.

## Tools

## Reproduction

`m = 223`, `n = 2m = 446`. With `a = {23, 26, 37, 95, 96, 116, 118, 151, 179, 189, 220, 222}` and `b = {13, 39, 139, 163, 195, 198, 202, 210, 211, 218}` as exponent sets,

    H_X = [circ(a) | circ(b)],    H_Z = [circ(b)^T | circ(a)^T]

where `circ(s)[i, (i + e) mod m] = 1` for each `e` in `s`. The submitted `codes/446-76-50.json` carries both matrices row by row and both distance witnesses.
